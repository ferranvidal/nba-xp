nba-xp: a pre-game NBA margin model

The model predicts the final point margin of an NBA game before it starts. This page shows the data splits, the features, the decisions, and the validation results. It also shows where the model does not beat a simple baseline.

1What it predicts

The model predicts the margin of a game: the home team's final points minus the away team's final points. It predicts a Normal distribution, not one number. The mean of the distribution (mu) comes from an XGBoost regressor. The spread (sigma) is one fixed number for all games: points.

From that one distribution the model derives three probabilities. P(home win) is the chance that the margin is above 0. P(blowout) is the chance that the margin is 15 points or more in either direction. P(tight game) is the chance that the margin is 5 points or fewer in either direction.

Headline result on the test season

On the 2025-26 test season ( games), the model misses the margin by points on average. That is points better than the Elo baseline and points better than the home-only baseline. The edge over the Elo baseline is small. The 95% interval of the difference includes zero, so one test season cannot show that the edge is real.

Terms on this page

MAE (mean absolute error)
The average of |actual margin - mu|, in points. Lower is better.
Brier score
The average of (predicted probability - outcome)^2, where the outcome is 0 or 1. Lower is better. A model that always says 50% scores 0.25.
Development
The seasons 2015-16 to 2024-25. The model trains and the choices are made on these seasons only.
Fold, out-of-fold prediction
A fold is one train/validate split. An out-of-fold prediction is a prediction for a game by a model that trained only on earlier seasons.
Test season
The 2025-26 season. No choice was made on it. The replay predicts it one game day at a time.
Elo rating, Elo baseline
The Elo rating is a strength number per team, used as a feature. The Elo baseline is a model: a straight line from the home-minus-away Elo rating to the margin, fitted on the training games.
Home-only baseline
A model that predicts the same margin (the average training margin, + points) for every game.
Champion chain, frozen v1
In the replay, the champion chain is the model that the replay may retrain and replace. Frozen v1 is the development model that is never retrained. If no check promotes a challenger, the two are the same model.
Game day
A calendar day with at least one game.

2Time split

The model never sees a game from the future of the game it predicts. Three rules enforce this, one at each level of the system. The diagram below shows the second and third rule. It is the most important diagram on this page, because a wrong split would make every other number meaningless.

  1. As-of rule (features). A feature for a game on date D uses only games with a game date before D. Games on the same day do not count. A test checks this rule: removing all games on or after D changes no feature for games on D.
  2. Forward chaining (development). Each fold trains on all seasons before one season and validates on that season. There is no random split.
  3. Untouched test season (evaluation). The 2025-26 season is never used to tune or to choose. The replay predicts it one game day at a time.
  • Warm-up. The seasons 2013-14 and 2014-15 only feed the Elo rating and the form features, so these features are mature when training starts in 2015-16.
  • Bubble. games from 2020-07-01 on (the restart in Orlando) are not training rows. They still update the Elo rating and the form features.
  • Final model. The model that goes to the test season trains on all training games of 2015-16 to 2024-25. The four folds are used to choose settings and sigma, not to build the final model.

3Features

The model has input columns from 10 feature families. Most families give three columns per quantity: one for the home team (home_), one for the away team (away_), and the home value minus the away value (diff_). The strongest columns are the diff_ ones: diff_win_pct, diff_elo and diff_wins.

Each chart below uses the training games (2015-16 to 2024-25, bubble excluded). It plots the mean home margin of the games in each bin of the home-minus-away value. The bars are 95% intervals of the mean. A flat line means the feature alone does not move the margin.

Which features carry the signal

Two measures answer this question, and they do not agree for every family. Gain is how much the splits on a column reduce the training error, summed over all trees. It shows what the trees use. Removal cost is the rise in out-of-fold MAE when I retrain the model without the family. It shows what the model needs to predict new games.

A warehouse adds cost and a server to run. Parquet keeps the same folder layout on disk and in the bucket gs://nba-data. The staged and feature files are MB together. One distribution gives all three probabilities, and they stay consistent with each other. One sigma is one number to estimate from 5,283 out-of-fold games.
Use a shallow XGBoost model chosen from a grid of 12 settings Deeper trees. A neural network. A linear model. The signal is weak and the data is small. A small grid limits how much the choice fits the noise of the validation seasons.
Use the Elo baseline as the model to beat Compare only with the home-only baseline. Compare with betting lines. Elo is a standard simple strength model for the NBA. A model that cannot beat it adds little. Betting lines are not in the snapshot.
Count a team as eliminated when it cannot reach 10th place in its conference (8th before 2020-21) Always use 8th place. Compute tiebreakers exactly. The play-in started in 2020-21. Teams in 7th to 10th place compete for the last two playoff spots, so a team is out only when it cannot reach 10th. The check ignores tiebreakers, so the flag can lag by one game.
new Promote a retrained model only when a paired bootstrap on the last 30 days says it is better with probability at least 0.9, on MAE and on Brier score Promote when the challenger has a lower MAE and Brier score (earlier rule). Never retrain. Retrain on a schedule with no check. The challenger and the champion differ by tiny amounts on about 200 games. A rule that compares two numbers promotes on noise. The bootstrap resamples the per-game differences 1,000 times and asks how often the challenger wins. I designed this rule after I saw the first replay of 2025-26, where the earlier rule promoted 8 of 18 times. So the replay is a clean test of the development model, but not a blind test of this rule.
new Compare recent games with the training games at the same point of the season, and leave out the game-type flags Compare with all training games (earlier rule). No drift monitor. Some features change by design during a season. Win and loss counts are low in October and high in April. A fixed reference flags them at every check, so the flags carry no information. I also chose this reference after I saw the first replay of 2025-26.
Replay the 2025-26 season day by day instead of ingesting live games A live feed with a scheduler. A live feed needs an always-on scheduler and a source that updates. That costs money. This is a personal project with no company infrastructure to run it on. The replay uses the same code path as a live job would: it receives one day of results, then predicts the next day.
No data-quality checks on the snapshot Schema, range and duplicate checks at staging. The owner of the data judged the snapshot sound, and it is frozen. Checks are worth more for a source that keeps changing. The pipeline runs no checks, so a bad row would pass through silently. The repository has unit tests for the as-of rule and for one hand-checked example per feature family.

6Test season replay (2025-26)

  • A retrain check runs on the first game day of each month, and when the rolling 30-day MAE rises more than 1 point above the development MAE (at most one such check per 7 days). No check runs in the first 30 days.
  • A check trains a challenger on all games before D minus 30 days. It scores the champion and the challenger on the 30 days before D. The promotion gate then resamples the games of that window 1,000 times. It promotes the challenger only if the challenger has the lower MAE in at least 90% of the resamples, and also the lower Brier score in at least 90% of the resamples.
  • Sigma and the XGBoost settings stay fixed at the development values for every model version.

7Monitoring: feature drift

At each retrain check the replay compares every input column in the last 30 days with a reference group of games. The measure is the PSI (population stability index). The replay cuts the reference values into 10 equal-count bins. It then counts which share of the recent games falls into each bin. The PSI is large when these shares differ much from the expected 10% per bin.

The replay flags a column when its PSI is above 0.2. In plain words: below 0.1 the column looks the same, between 0.1 and 0.2 it moved a little, and above 0.2 it moved enough that the recent games look like a different population. The threshold is a rule of thumb from credit scoring. It is not a statistical test, and the model can still predict well when a column is flagged.

8Architecture

The pipeline runs on a laptop today. The cloud layer is written as code, but I have not applied it. Nothing on this page claims that a job or an API runs in the cloud.

  • Built and run on a laptop: snapshot export, staging, features, training with MLflow (SQLite backend), the replay, the CI workflow, and the unit tests. The bucket gs://nba-data exists and holds the raw snapshot.
  • Built and run on a laptop: the API and the exported serving model. The API serves the 2025-26 games through GET /games/{date} and POST /predict.
  • Planned, not deployed yet: the Cloud Run job and service, Artifact Registry, the two service accounts, Workload Identity Federation, and the deploy workflow. This page itself is static and needs none of them.

9Limits

  • The edge over Elo is small. In development the model lowers MAE by points (95% interval to ). On the test season the gain is points, and the interval includes zero. A user would not feel this difference in single games.
  • The model does not know who plays. It has no injury, lineup or rest-decision data. Player data enters only through the minutes of the 8 most-used players in the last 10 games.
  • No betting lines. A line already contains injury and lineup news, so it is a stronger benchmark than Elo. The snapshot has no lines, so I made no comparison.
  • The blowout and tight-game probabilities say little. P(blowout) ranges only from about 0.28 to 0.44 between games, and P(tight game) from about 0.20 to 0.28. Their Brier scores are close to the home-only baseline (blowout against ; tight against ).
  • One sigma is too narrow for some games. Playoff games have a larger residual spread than regular-season games. On the test season the RMSE () is above sigma (), so the model is overconfident there.
  • One test season is one sample. The 2025-26 results come from games of one season. The development MAE changes from fold to fold by up to points.
  • Neutral sites. Only the NBA Cup final and the 2020 bubble count as neutral. Games abroad and other neutral-site games count as home games.
  • Arena data. Travel distance uses one arena location per franchise, typed from general knowledge. Moves inside a city (Warriors 2019, Clippers 2024) are ignored.
  • Eliminated flag. The flag ignores tiebreakers, so it can lag by one game.
  • Not live. The snapshot ends with the 2025-26 season. The cloud layer is not deployed.
